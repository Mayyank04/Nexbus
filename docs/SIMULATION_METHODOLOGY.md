# NEXBUS — Counterfactual Simulation Methodology

## 1. Overview & Epistemological Stance

The NEXBUS Simulation Lab (`/simulate`) allows transport planners and municipal authorities to test policy hypotheticals (fleet electrification, headway compression, transit signal priority) without physically disrupting the live city.

**Epistemological Integrity Rule:**
All outputs generated by this engine are strictly classified as **HYPOTHETICAL**. They represent mathematical projections based on queueing theory and empirical elasticity factors, and are never presented as observed historical improvements.

---

## 2. Governing Mathematical Formulations

### Equation 1: Expected Commuter Platform Wait Time (Little's Law Extension)
Commuter arrival at high-frequency transit stops is modeled as a Poisson arrival process with headway irregularity (Welding, 1957; Osuna & Newell, 1972):

$$\mathbb{E}[W] = \frac{H}{2} \cdot \left(1 + C_v^2\right) - \Delta_{\text{signal}}$$

Where:
- $H$: Scheduled bus headway in minutes (e.g. 12.0 min baseline, 6.0 min proposed).
- $C_v$: Coefficient of variation of bus headways ($C_v \approx 0.35$ under typical Delhi mixed traffic conditions).
- $\Delta_{\text{signal}}$: Delay reduction credit from Transit Signal Priority (1.4 minutes when enabled).

### Equation 2: Induced Passenger Demand (Transit Elasticity Operator)
When passenger wait time is compressed, commuters shift from two-wheelers, auto-rickshaws, and private cars onto the bus network according to transit demand elasticity:

$$\frac{\Delta Q}{Q_0} = \epsilon_w \cdot \frac{\Delta W}{W_0}$$

Where:
- $\epsilon_w$: Commuter wait-time elasticity ($\epsilon_w = -0.38$ nominal benchmark; Litman 2021, TRB 2004).
- $\Delta W / W_0$: Fractional change in average platform wait time.

### Equation 3: Environmental Emissions Offset (ARAI / CPCB Factors)
Displacing standard 12-meter CNG combustion bus miles with electric bus operations avoids direct tailpipe emissions (CPCB/PROBES/136):

$$\text{Avoided CO}_2\text{ (Tons/Month)} = \frac{N_{\text{active}} \times D_{\text{daily}} \times 30 \times EF_{\text{cng}}}{1000}$$

Where:
- $N_{\text{active}} = N_{\text{requested}} \times \alpha_{\text{depot}}$: Effective deployed buses accounting for depot availability rate ($\alpha_{\text{depot}} = 85\%$).
- $D_{\text{daily}} = 180\text{ km/day}$: Average commercial daily distance per DTC bus.
- $EF_{\text{cng}} = 1.120\text{ kg CO}_2/\text{km}$: Direct tailpipe emissions factor for 12m urban CNG buses.
- $EF_{\text{nox}} = 0.014\text{ kg NOx/km}$: Nitrous oxide tailpipe emissions factor.

## 3. Parametric Sensitivity Analysis

NEXBUS computes sensitivity envelopes across two key operational dimensions:

1. **Demand Elasticity Variation ($\epsilon_w \in [-0.20, -0.30, -0.38, -0.50, -0.65]$):**
   Demonstrates how projected passenger load factor shifts from seated equilibrium ($<85\%$) to standees ($>100\%$) if commuter price-sensitivity or mode-shift willingness increases.
2. **Depot Availability Rate ($\alpha_{\text{depot}} \in [70\%, 80\%, 85\%, 90\%, 95\%]$):**
   Reflects real-world charger maintenance and battery turnaround at Rajghat and Rohini depots.
